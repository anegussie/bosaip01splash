# Option 20 V5: the 10G cap, two lanes, and the memory margin the governor actually requires

Measured 2026-11-04 on the MacBook Air (M5, 10 cores, 16 GB unified memory), model
`ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF:IQ3_XXS` with the DFlash2 draft, served on port 1235 as
`qwen3.8-flash-next-iq3-xxs-splash`.

```sh
SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_MAX_MEMORY=10G SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_MAX_ACTIVE_REQUESTS=2 \
SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_SECOND_LANE_BELOW_GATE=1 bash /Users/mymac/startllamacpp.sh --direct 20
```

Engine argv: `131072 10737418240 17179869184 --persistent-cache on --cache-dir
~/Library/Caches/Splash/flash-next-option20-iq3-xxs --prefill-chunk-tokens 1024 --state-format bf16
--resource-wait-timeout 60 --ple-memory 67108864`. The second lane is a server-side setting, not an
engine flag: `/status` reports `transport.max_active_requests: 2`, and the launcher announced
`Native inference concurrency: 2`. Context stayed 131,072 and `--expert-memory` stayed `auto`.

Rows come from `build/validation/option20-v5-20261104/run_v5.py` (the V4 harness copied forward), with
fresh `cache_salt` per cold row, the repeat row holding that salt fixed, 10 s cooldowns, count-to-30
and ORCHID probes, the three-request `lane-probe`, and the `parent-child` probe sampling `/status`
every half second. Condensed data: `dev/benchmarks/qwen38-option20-v5-20261104.json`.

## The margin V4's prediction got wrong

The V4 write-up expected `growth_allowed` to reopen as soon as `host_headroom_bytes` was above zero.
The code asks for more than that:

```cpp
// runtime/engine/MemoryGovernor.cpp:301
bool hostGrowthAllowed = systemPressure_ == MemoryPressure::Normal &&
    !growthHeld(host) && hostHeadroom >= kHostWarningMarginBytes;

// runtime/engine/MemoryGovernor.hpp:85-86
inline constexpr uint64_t kHostWarningMarginBytes = 1ULL << 30;   // growth resumes at 1 GiB
inline constexpr uint64_t kHostRecoveryMarginBytes = 2ULL << 30;   // reopen after pressure
```

`host_headroom_bytes` is the host's spare memory above `host_spare_reserve_bytes` (2.147 GiB on this
16 GB Mac). Growth therefore needs **3.147 GiB of spare RAM**, and after a pressure episode it needs
4.147 GiB to reopen. Measured at the 10G cap:

| Moment | host spare | host headroom | `growth_allowed` |
|---|---|---|---|
| idle, six samples over 30 s | 2.20-2.27 GiB | 0.20-0.27 GiB | false |
| during the parent's prefill | 2.95 GiB | 0.75 GiB | false |
| end of run (`charged` 8.36 GiB, cap headroom 2.38 GiB) | 2.26 GiB | 0.26 GiB | false |

The gap is not large: about 0.9 GiB at idle, about 0.3 GiB during prefill. But it is a gap on the
host's side of the ledger, not the Metal cap's. Lowering the cap from 11G to 10G bought 0.37 GiB of
host headroom (0.0 to 0.26 GiB) and nothing more, because the engine's own footprint is dominated by
the 7.92 GiB of fixed runtime reservation, not by the headroom under the cap.

## Two ways to close the remaining gap

Neither needs a smaller context.

**Free host memory.** The machine's other tenants, measured with `top` while the engine was idle:
Google Chrome plus its helpers 1.42 GiB, LM Studio 0.39 GiB, cmux 0.37 GiB, a Python process 0.31
GiB, Activity Monitor 0.29 GiB, opencode 0.26 GiB. Chrome alone is more than the 0.9 GiB the governor
wants. Swap is 2.15 of 3.07 GiB used, so part of that 0.9 GiB is already paged out and would come back
as cost. This is testable with no restart: quit the heaviest app, then re-run `parent-child` and read
`host_headroom_bytes` and `decode_batches_by_width`.

**Lower the cap further.** `SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_MAX_MEMORY=9G` is inside the launcher's accepted range
and should move another ~1 GiB to the host. The risk is the other side of the plan: V5's cold prefill
already prefers the smaller cap, but 9G cuts into the resident KV allowance that keeps a 28,589-token
prompt in RAM, and the profile's whole 131,072 window is what the spill plan is sized against. That
trade is what a 9G row would measure.
