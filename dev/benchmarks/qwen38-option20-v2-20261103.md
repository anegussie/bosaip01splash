# Option 20 V2: an explicit 2 GiB expert store changed nothing (2026-11-03)

Launch under test:

```
build/splash serve-native .../2f948307... 131072 11811160064 8589934592
  --persistent-cache on --cache-dir ~/Library/Caches/Splash/flash-next-option20-iq3-xxs
  --prefill-chunk-tokens 1024 --state-format bf16 --resource-wait-timeout 60
  --ple-memory 67108864 --expert-memory 2147483648
```

`11811160064` is `SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_MAX_MEMORY=11G` clamped to the engine's own
ceiling, and `--expert-memory 2147483648` is the `2G` request. Rows:
`build/validation/option20-v1-20261103/run_v2.py`, all `clean: true`.

## The store did not change size

The startup log said so directly:

```
Routed experts stream from their files: 512 slots for 24576 experts (2%) in 1262 MiB,
a store of 1992 MiB in 1 chunks, all that the floors, its own among them, leave it
beside the KV kept resident; it gives none back, its floor, the 2048 MiB
--expert-memory sets (1992 MiB in whole slots and chunks), being all of it.
```

`/status` agrees: `cap_bytes` 2,089,468,688 = `floor_bytes` 2,089,468,688, 512 of
24,576 slots, `chunks 1`, `shrinks 0`, `regrows 0`, 31 to 42 resident, `hit_rate`
0.00. That is the same store `auto` produced at the 10 GiB cap.

`--expert-memory` is documented as the **least** RAM for the routed experts, and
`runtime/engine/MemoryPlan.cpp:246` treats an explicit value as that minimum
(`plan.expertBytes = experts.charged`), while only `auto` adds what the plan
leaves over. An explicit size therefore fixes the store at its floor, so there are
no elastic chunks to take back or give back.

Against V1 (28,589 tokens: 181,393-191,996 ms at 148.9-157.6 tok/s; decode
11.1-12.2 tok/s), nothing moved. `hit_rate` stayed 0.00, so the store cached
nothing worth caching. The disk prefix cache still works: the repeated prompt read
28,576 cached tokens and prefilled 13.

## Where the memory actually goes

`/status` `memory_plan.budget` on this 16 GB Mac at the 11 G cap:

| Field | Bytes |
|---|---|
| physical_memory_bytes | 17,179,869,184 |
| recommended_working_set_bytes | 12,713,115,648 |
| hard_budget_bytes (ceiling the engine uses) | 11,639,373,824 |
| target_weights_bytes | 5,665,555,216 |
| draft_weights_bytes | 523,436,032 |
| fixed_runtime_bytes (weights, stores, reserves) | 7,922,135,964 |
| dynamic_budget_bytes (what stores compete for) | 3,717,237,860 |
| kv_capacity_pages / kv_capacity_bytes | 8,192 / 3,472,883,712 |
| kv_capacity_tokens | 262,144 |

The whole dynamic budget is 3.46 GiB and the resident KV pool alone takes 3.23
GiB of it. Experts and resident KV compete for that same 3.46 GiB, and resident
KV wins by default: experts sit at their floor and KV fills the rest. Raising
`--expert-memory` past the leftover is the way to take that space back, at the
price of a smaller resident KV pool, which the SSD tier now absorbs.

## Next row, V3

```sh
SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_MAX_MEMORY=11G SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_EXPERT_MEMORY=4G \
SPLASH_FLASH_NEXT_MAX_CACHE_DISK=16G bash /Users/mymac/startllamacpp.sh --direct 20
```

Two reasons for `4G`: the store grows in whole slots and chunks, so 2 GiB bought
512 slots and the next useful size is about 1,024 slots (4 GiB at 3.89 MiB per
slot). And the 8 GiB disk quota is now full (`used_bytes` 8,547,762,176 of
8,589,934,592 after a few benchmark prompts), so the prefix cache needs room to
keep more than a handful of requests. The volume has about 72 GiB free and the
engine keeps 10 GiB of it spare, so 16 GiB of quota fits.

Accept when the startup log shows a store whose cap is above its floor with more
than 512 slots, `regrows` greater than zero, `hit_rate` above 0.01, and decode
above 13 tok/s, with `memory_pressure` no worse than `warning`. Watch the KV side
in the same run: a smaller resident KV pool should not raise cold 28k prefill
above about 200 seconds or hurt the repeated-prompt 1.2 second start.
